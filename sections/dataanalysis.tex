\chapter{Data Analysis} \label{cha:data}
This chapter presents the \acs{ReID} and \acs{SR} datasets used in this project. The selected \acs{ReID} datasets represent realistic surveillance conditions and are established datasets within person \acs{ReID} research, ensuring comparability across studies \cite{ZhouSurvey2021}. Furthermore, two \acs{SR} datasets have been selected and presented.

\section{Overview of ReID Datasets}
\label{sec:reid-dataset}

Datasets were chosen based on open academic access, realistic surveillance conditions, well-defined train/query/gallery splits, multiple viewpoints per identity, and resolution variation. All datasets provide images cropped to bounding boxes around the person, making it directly usable for training and testing of \acs{ReID} models. \subsection*{Market-1501}

Market-1501 is one of the most widely used datasets for person \acs{ReID}. It is recorded at Tsinghua University in Beijing using six surveillance cameras: five in \acf{HD} and one in \acf{SD} resolution. The dataset contains 32,668 cropped images of 1,501 unique individuals, each appearing in at least two camera views. Bounding boxes are generated automatically with a \acf{DPM}, and the resulting person crops are resized to a fixed resolution of \textit{128x64} pixels. In the released dataset, all images are therefore provided at this standardized resolution of \textit{128x64} pixels. Because the detections are automatic, some bounding boxes are misaligned or partially cropped, which makes the dataset realistic for providing material under real world surveillance conditions \cite{Zheng2015ICCV}.

\subsection*{DukeMTMC-ReID}

\acf{DukeMTMC} is from the Duke Multi-Target Multi-Camera project and was recorded on the Duke University campus using eight 1080p cameras. The dataset contains 36,411 person images divided into 702 training and 702 test identities. 
The data capture diverse viewpoints, lighting variations, and scales from close-up pedestrians to distant, low-resolution figures which provides realistic footage from security cameras where the distance to the person can differ \cite{Gou2017DukeMTMC4ReID}. This diversity strengthens evaluation across different camera positions and environmental conditions.

\subsection*{CUHK03}

\acf{CUHK03} was collected at The Chinese University of Hong Kong and was one of the first \acs{ReID} datasets large enough to support deep learning \cite{KaggleCUHK03}. It consists of approximately 14,000 images of about 1,467 individuals captured by ten cameras, which are grouped into five disjoint camera pairs.  Each person appears in two cameras, giving two different viewpoints for every identity.  The dataset is released in two versions: a labeled set with manually annotated bounding boxes, and a detected set generated by the same \acf{DPM} detector used in Market-1501 \cite{Li2014DeepReID}. This project only uses the labeled set, although \acs{CUHK03} contains a smaller amount of images than Market-1501 and \acs{DukeMTMC}, its high quality annotations and structured format still make it valuable for training.

\subsection*{MSMT17}

\acf{MSMT17} was recorded by a network of 15 surveillance cameras, on an university campus, deployed across indoor and outdoor environments, capturing video footage over an extended period. The dataset was designed to address real-world challenges in person \acs{ReID} including complex lighting variations, diverse viewpoint changes, and the scale requirements of large camera networks. It contains 126,441 annotated bounding boxes of 4,101 unique identities. Pedestrian bounding boxes are detected automatically using a Faster R-CNN detector, and the resulting person crops varies in size. The raw videos were captured during 12 different time slots throughout the day, spanning about 180 hours of footage in total. This temporal coverage results in lighting variations, ranging from bright daylight to low light evening conditions, which closely mirrors the operational conditions of real-world surveillance systems. The combination of indoor and outdoor scenes, long-duration recording, and diverse environmental conditions enables \acs{ReID} models trained on \acs{MSMT17} to better generalize to real-world surveillance scenarios with varying lighting, viewpoints, and scale \cite{wei2018person}.

\subsection*{ILIDS-VIDS}

The \acf{iLIDS-VID} dataset is captured in an airport arrival hall under a multi camera CCTV network. The dataset consists of 600 image sequences capturing 300 distinct individuals, with one pair of image sequences from two non overlapping camera views for each person. The dataset is provided in two versions, a static images version where single frames are randomly selected from each sequence, and an image sequences version for video-based \acs{ReID} evaluation. For this project, the static image based version will be used to maintain consistency with the other datasets. \acs{iLIDS-VID} is considered highly challenging due to several factors like clothing similarities among individuals, significant lighting and viewpoint variations across camera views, cluttered backgrounds with heavy occlusion and the airport environment. These characteristics make the dataset particularly valuable for evaluating \acs{ReID} methods under realistic surveillance conditions. \cite{wang2014person}.

\section{Overview of Super Resolution Datasets} \label{sec: superresdata}
The datasets for \acs{SR} training were chosen based on availability and aligned \acs{HR} and \acs{LR} images pairs, diversity in content, and relevance of the \acs{ReID} task. \acs{DIV2K} provides high quality images with synthetic degradation, while \acs{RELLISUR} offers real optical degradations, addressing the domain gap discussed in section \ref{sec:SR-data}. 

\subsection*{Div2K}

\acf{DIV2K} was introduced as part of the NTIRE 2017 Challenge on Single Image Super-Resolution \cite{Agustsson2017NTIRE}. The dataset consists of 1,000 high-quality RGB images, which have all been manually collected from the Internet, with special attention to image quality, diversity of sources, content variety, and copyright considerations. The dataset has been split into three parts: 800 images for training, 100 images for validation, and 100 images for testing, all at 2K resolution. For each \acs{HR} image, corresponding \acs{LR} images are provided at downscaling factors of \textit{×2}, \textit{×3}, and \textit{×4}, which are generated using bicubic interpolation. Additionally, an unknown degradation track provides \acs{LR} images that are created with hidden degradation operators to  simulate realistic scenarios. The images were selected to achieve a balanced distribution across visual content bit-per-pixel compression rates, and feature diverse content such as natural scenes, textures, objects, and patterns. This diversity and high quality make \acs{DIV2K} widely applied in benchmarks used to train and evaluate \acs{SR} algorithms \cite{Agustsson2017NTIRE}.

\subsection*{RELLISUR}

\acf{RELLISUR} was collected by Aalborg University with a focus on image resolution and not digital downsampling. Each of the 850 scenes is recorded in 3 different scales. \textit{x1} scale, \textit{x2} scale, and \textit{x4} scale, which are created by changing the focal length on the camera. This means that the differences in resolution originate from the optical settings, and are not produced by bicubic manipulation. The dataset contains both a low light and a normal light version. This project uses only the normal light version, consisting of 2,550 images split into training, validation, and test sets. \acs{RELLISUR} covers indoor and outdoor environments, which both includes different structures, materials, and distances to enhance usability in real-world scenarios \cite{aakerberg2021rellisur}.

\section{Summary}

 This chapter presented the datasets that will be used for both the \acs{ReID} and \acs{SR} tasks. The \acs{ReID} datasets cover a range of realistic surveillance conditions, such as multiple viewpoints per identity, occlusions, and resolution variations. For the \acs{SR} task, two datasets were selected: one with synthetic degradation and one with real optical degradation, to provide complementary data for training.
