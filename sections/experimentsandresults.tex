\chapter{Experiments and Results} \label{cha: experiments and results}
 This chapter presents the experiments conducted to evaluate \hyperlink{hypothesis:1}{\textit{Hypothesis 1}} and \hyperlink{hypothesis:2}{\textit{Hypothesis 2}} as defined in Section \ref{sec:hypotheses}. The objective is to determine whether incorporating an \acs{SR} module improves the robustness of a person \acs{ReID} module with the \acs{InD} and \acs{OOD} conditions in mind. To support this evaluation, this chapter first presents an independent assessment of the chosen \acs{SR} model, followed by the four models defined in Section \ref{sec:model_definitions}: the \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} model, the \hyperlink{model:augmented}{Augmented} model, the \hyperlink{model:sequential}{Sequential} model, and the \hyperlink{model:endtoend}{End-to-end} model. The results from these experiments are presented and briefly discussed. 

\section{SR Experiments} \label{sec: srexperiments}
Before integrating \acs{SR} into the \acs{ReID} pipeline, the \acs{EDSR} architecture is evaluated independently to verify that it provides meaningful improvements over simple interpolation methods and to determine which training strategy produces the best results for real-world degradations. This section presents the training results, a comparison between scratch and finetuned models, and a direct comparison against bicubic interpolation.

\subsubsection{Test Set Evaluation}
Two \acs{EDSR} models were trained to compare different training strategies: one trained from scratch exclusively on \acs{RELLISUR}, and one finetuned on \acs{DIV2K} starting from the best scratch checkpoint. Both models were evaluated on the \acs{RELLISUR} test set consisting of 85 naturally degraded, low-resolution images. Table \ref{tab:edsr_test_results} presents the test set performance for both models. The scratch model achieved a mean \acs{PSNR} of 28.25 dB and \acs{SSIM} of 0.9751, whereas the finetuned model reached 28.09 dB \acs{PSNR} and 0.9733 \acs{SSIM}. The scratch model outperformed the finetuned model by 0.16 dB in \acs{PSNR} and 0.0018 in \acs{SSIM}.

\subsubsection{Analysis of SR Results}
The \acs{SR} experiments reveal several important findings regarding both model performance and training strategy effectiveness. The scratch model trained on \acs{RELLISUR} achieved a \acs{PSNR} of 28.25 dB, which represents a meaningful improvement of 1.10 dB over bicubic interpolation (27.15 dB). The high \acs{SSIM} value of 0.9751 indicates that the model preserves structural details effectively, which is favorable for downstream \acs{ReID} tasks that rely on discriminative features such as clothing patterns and textures. These results confirm that the domain-specific training approach produced a functional \acs{SR} model capable of outperforming traditional interpolation methods.
\\\\
However, contrary to expectations, finetuning on \acs{DIV2K} degraded performance rather than improving it. The finetuned model achieved 0.16 dB lower \acs{PSNR} and 0.0018 lower \acs{SSIM} compared to the scratch model. The validation \acs{PSNR} dropped from 24.72 dB after scratch training to 24.28 dB after finetuning, with the best epoch occurring immediately at epoch 1, indicating that continued training on synthetic data was detrimental. This performance degradation can be attributed to the domain gap between \acs{RELLISUR} and \acs{DIV2K}. \acs{RELLISUR} contains real optical degradations caused by physical factors such as focal length and sensor characteristics, while \acs{DIV2K} uses synthetic bicubic downsampling. When the model was finetuned on \acs{DIV2K}, it adapted to synthetic degradation patterns that do not match the realistic degradations in the test data. This phenomenon, sometimes referred to as negative transfer, caused the model to partially forget the realistic degradation patterns learned during scratch training.
\\\\
The observation that domain-specific training outperforms transfer learning when the target domain differs significantly from the source domain validates the importance of matching training data characteristics to the deployment scenario. For this project, the scratch model was therefore selected for integration with the \acs{ReID} pipeline in subsequent experiments.


\subsubsection{EDSR vs. Bicubic Interpolation}
To verify that \acs{EDSR} provides meaningful improvements over simple interpolation methods, as described in \hyperlink{req:1}{\textit{Requirement 1}}, a direct comparison was conducted between the trained \acs{EDSR} models and bicubic interpolation.
\\\\
Both \acs{EDSR} models and bicubic interpolation were evaluated on the same \acs{RELLISUR} test set. Table \ref{tab:edsr_vs_bicubic} presents the comparison results. The scratch \acs{EDSR} model achieved a mean \acs{PSNR} of 28.25 dB compared to 27.15 dB for bicubic interpolation, representing an improvement of 1.10 dB. The finetuned \acs{EDSR} model achieved 28.09 dB, representing an improvement of 0.94 dB over bicubic interpolation. For \acs{SSIM}, the scratch model achieved 0.9751 compared to 0.9689 for bicubic (improvement of 0.0061), and the finetuned model achieved 0.9733 (improvement of 0.0044). These results confirm that both \acs{EDSR} models consistently outperform bicubic interpolation, with the scratch model providing the largest improvement.

\section{Out-of-Distribution Test} \label{sec: oodtestny}

This \acs{OOD} test was conducted using the test set from \acs{DukeMTMC} under three different conditions, as well as the entire \acs{iLIDS-VID} dataset, which is unseen by \acs{OSNet}. An overview is presented in Table \ref{tab:newood_conditions}.
\\\\
The test on \acs{DukeMTMC} uses 2,228 query images and 17,661 gallery images, while \acs{iLIDS-VID} uses the 300 images from camera 1 as query and the other 300 images from camera 2 as the gallery. This larger test set directly improves the representativeness of the evaluation. Since \acs{iLIDS-VID} contains only two images per identity, the evaluation remains a one-shot setting. In contrast, \acs{DukeMTMC} includes multiple gallery images per identity, making it a multi-shot evaluation. Therefore, \acs{DukeMTMC} was evaluated using Rank-\textit{k} accuracy and \acs{mAP}, while \acs{iLIDS-VID} was evaluated using only Rank-\textit{k} accuracy.

\section{Experiments} \label{sec: experimentsdefined}

In order to determine whether implementing an \acs{SR} module in the \acs{ReID} pipeline improves performance, several experiments were conducted to establish a clear and fair comparison. The experiments are designed to test the hypotheses defined in Section \ref{sec:hypotheses}. 
\\\\
Before incorporating the \acs{SR} module, the effects of applying strong data augmentation during training is foremost evaluated to determine whether robustness to \acs{OOD} conditions can be achieved through training strategies alone. This step provides a reference point to establish whether the additional complexity introduced by the \acs{SR} module is justified. 

\subsection{Experiment One: Augmented Model} \label{sec: baselineosnet}

This experiment was conducted to evaluate \hyperlink{hypothesis:1}{\textit{Hypothesis 1}}, which states that training a person \acs{ReID} module on augmented data will improve robustness under \acs{OOD} conditions.  
\\\\
Two models were compared: the \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} model and the \hyperlink{model:augmented}{Augmented} model. Both models share the same architecture, training datasets, and training procedures, ensuring that the applied augmentation strategy is the only inconsistency that can affect performance. Evaluations were conducted using the \acs{OOD} test as described in Section \ref{sec: oodtestny}. 
\\\\
This experiment establishes whether robustness to \acs{OOD} conditions can be achieved through training strategies alone, without introducing additional components to the \acs{ReID} module. 
 

\subsection{Experiment Two: \acs{EDSR} and Baseline Combined} \label{sec: sr+baseline}

This experiment was conducted to evaluate \hyperlink{hypothesis:2}{\textit{Hypothesis 2}}, by investigating if adding \acs{EDSR} as a preprocessing step improves \acs{ReID} performance under \acs{OOD} conditions. 
\\\\
Two model configurations were compared: the \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} model and a sequentially combined \acs{EDSR} + \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} configuration, where images are first preprocessed by the \acs{SR} module and subsequently passed to the \acs{ReID} module. The \acs{ReID} model architecture, training data, and training procedures remained identical to the ones used for the \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} configuration.
\\\\
In this setup, with the \acs{EDSR} and \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} modules combined without joint end-to-end training, the effect of \acs{SR} as a preprocessing step on \acs{ReID} performance can be evaluated more directly. 


\subsection{Experiment Three: End-to-end \acs{EDSR} and Baseline}

This experiment was conducted to further evaluate \hyperlink{hypothesis:2}{Hypothesis 2} by assessing if joint end-to-end training of the \acs{SR} and \acs{ReID} module improves robustness under \acs{OOD} conditions.
\\\\
The \hyperlink{model:endtoend}{End-to-end} model is compared against the \hyperlink{Sequential}{Sequential} model. Both configurations used the same \acs{EDSR} and \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} model, with the only difference being the training strategy. While the \hyperlink{model:sequential}{Sequential} model is combined, the \hyperlink{model:endtoend}{End-to-end} model trained the \acs{EDSR} and \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} models jointly, allowing the \acs{SR} module to adapt specifically to the \acs{ReID} task.
\\\\
By keeping the other factors constant, this experiment isolated the effect of joint end-to-end training, compared to the sequential integration.

\subsection{Summary of Results}

The experiments confirmed that image resolution and compression significantly affect person \acs{ReID} performance. The \hyperlink{model:baseline}{Baseline} model achieved the highest performance compared to the three other model configurations, particularly under \acs{InD} and \acs{DukeMTMC} \acs{OOD} conditions. 
\\\\
However, the \hyperlink{model:entoend}{End-to-end} model showed promising results in the cross-domain \acs{OOD} test, achieving the highest Rank-1 out of all the models, which supports the idea that integrating \acs{SR} into \acs{ReID} can improve generalization to unseen datasets. 
\\\\
The \hyperlink{model:augmented}{Augmented} and \hyperlink{model:sequential}{Sequential} models consistently achieve the lowest results out of the four, suggesting that data augmentation and simple pre-processing with \acs{SR} is insufficient without proper integration.
